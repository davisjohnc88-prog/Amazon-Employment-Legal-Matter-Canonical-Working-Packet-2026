## Substack seeds: the first three articles

Each seed has a working title, a hook, an outline, and a **prompt for you** to write from. The details from your timeline go here, not on the slides.

### Part 1: Healing Everywhere But Work
**Hook:** "I thought I was still healing from a breakup. Then everything healed except one place."
**Outline:**
1. The breakup, the court case, and the flying monkeys. Keep it brief, as context only.
2. The healing work you did, and what changed at home, with friends, and on days off.
3. The bar that wouldn't move: work. Give two or three dated moments from your timeline.
4. Naming it: gaslighting, credibility laundering, and institutional betrayal (Smith & Freyd, 2014).
5. What you do now: keep your own record, plan the next 10 minutes.

**Your prompt:** *Pick the first moment you remember thinking, "Wait, this is coming from work, not from me." Where were you? What was said, word for word, as best you remember? What did your body do? Write it as a scene first, then explain what it meant.*

### Part 2: The Least Believed
**Hook:** Open with the memoir line: "I was the youngest. The least believed…"
**Outline:**
1. Youngest of nine. Your closest sibling is eight years older. How the scapegoat role worked in your family.
2. How a childhood like that teaches you to anticipate mistakes, and to give too many chances.
3. The same play on a new stage: family roles and workplace roles side by side.
4. Self-justification (Tavris & Aronson): why people would rather rewrite the story than admit a mistake.
5. What you're practicing now.

**Your prompt:** *Write about a time at work when you gave someone "one more chance." What did you hope would happen? What happened instead? Then ask yourself whose voice told you to wait, and where you first heard it.*

### Part 3: The Vocabulary Nobody Gave You
**Hook:** "In research on DARVO, people who learned how the tactic works were less fooled by it. So here are the words."
**Outline:** A field guide built from your existing vocabulary table (essay Part XV). For each term, give the plain definition, how it sounds at work, and one line from your own experience. Finish with documentation asymmetry and the quiet PIP.

**Your prompt:** *Pick the three terms from the poster that hit hardest. For each one, write one sentence that starts "The first time I saw this at work…"*

## Sources used in this batch

- Workplace Bullying Institute, [2024 U.S. Workplace Bullying Survey](https://workplacebullying.org/2024-wbi-us-survey/): 32.3% directly bullied, 52.2M workers, 74.8M affected, 72% aware, 75% bully acted alone, 55% bosses, 29% peers, 62% of targets lost their jobs vs. 27% of perpetrators facing consequences, 2% coworker intervention, 87% support for a new law.
- Quetelet (1835), Galton (1883), Binet and Simon (1905), *Buck v. Bell* (1927), the ADA (1990), Sinclair's "Don't Mourn for Us" (1993), and Milton (2012): all checked in `notes/factcheck/fact-check-full.md`, the report's fact check.
- Trundle et al. (2023), *Trauma, Violence, & Abuse*, [doi:10.1177/15248380221093689](https://doi.org/10.1177/15248380221093689): 34 studies; pooled 44% overall victimization, 47% bullying; high heterogeneity.
- Harsey & Freyd (2020), [doi:10.1080/10926771.2020.1774695](https://doi.org/10.1080/10926771.2020.1774695): DARVO lowers victim credibility, and a short explanation of DARVO reduces that effect.
- Smith & Freyd (2014), "Institutional betrayal," *American Psychologist* 69(6), [doi:10.1037/a0037564](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037564).
- Tavris & Aronson (2007), *Mistakes Were Made (But Not by Me)*, Harcourt.
- Term origins: Freyd (1997) for DARVO; Bateson et al. (1956) for the double bind; the 1938 play *Gas Light* for gaslighting.

The research items above were fact-checked earlier in the project (`notes/factcheck/fact-check-full.md`), and I checked the WBI numbers against WBI's own page today.
